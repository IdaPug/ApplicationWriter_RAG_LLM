These rules must be followed whenever generating or improving a job application.

Never invent work experience, education, qualifications, projects, skills, achievements, responsibilities, or results.
Only use information supported by my CV, previous applications, personal profile, or information explicitly provided by me.
Use an appropriate level of expertise.
If an important qualification or piece of information is missing, do not make assumptions. Ask me or leave it out.
Distinguish between demonstrated experience and transferable skills.
Never invent numerical results, performance improvements, or achievements.


Tailor every application to the specific job posting.
Identify the most important requirements and responsibilities in the job posting.
Prioritise experiences and skills that demonstrate my suitability for those requirements.
Do not simply repeat the job posting or list all my skills.
Explain how my experience is relevant to the employer's needs.
Explain how I will use relevant experience to do task at the company.
Avoid including irrelevant information just because it appears in my CV.



Write naturally and professionally. 
Avoid generic AI-sounding phrases, clichés, and exaggerated enthusiasm.
Be specific rather than vague.
Use confident language without sounding arrogant.
Avoid unnecessary adjectives and overly complicated sentences.
Avoid repeating the same information in different sections.
Maintain a coherent narrative throughout the application.
Make the application soiund punchy and personal.


Follow any length requirements specified in the job posting or application instructions.
Default cover letter length: approximately one page. 2400 characters
Use short paragraphs and clear transitions.
Make every paragraph contribute something relevant.
Do not add filler just to make the application longer.


Normally write in the language used in the job posting.
Use appropriate professional terminology for that language and industry.
Do not translate company names, product names, or technical terms unnecessarily.
Maintain consistent spelling and terminology.



Use previous applications as inspiration for tone, structure, and relevant examples.
Prefer examples that are relevant to the current position.
Do not copy previous applications blindly.
Do not reuse company-specific statements or references in applications to another company.
Previous application outcomes must not be treated as proof that the writing itself was successful.


Ensure the cover letter is consistent with the submitted CV.
Do not introduce claims in the cover letter that contradict the CV.
Do not repeat the entire CV in the cover letter.
Use the cover letter to explain motivation, relevance, and the connection between my experience and the position.


Before presenting an application, check:
Are all factual claims supported?
Does the application address the actual job requirements?
Is the writing specific to this employer and role?
Is the language natural and professional?
Is there unnecessary repetition?
Does the application follow the requested length and format?
Are there any invented details or unsupported claims?

If information is insufficient to write a convincing application without making assumptions, explain what is missing rather than inventing it.
